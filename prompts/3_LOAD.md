# Anchor — Step 3: Load and go

You do not need an AI for this step. This is Anchor itself.

---

## 1. Create a session

Give it a name you will recognise later. "Nexthink, AI Agent Engineer". "Client call, Acme, pricing".
Pick the kind: interview, client, team, investor, other. Pick the language you expect to be speaking — your cards can be in a different language; that is fine, Anchor matches meaning across languages.

## 2. Drop in your cards

The markdown file from Step 2. Anchor will:
- parse the cards,
- reject or offer to split anything over six bullets,
- warn about any bullet that reads like prose (that means you accidentally built a script, and a script will fail you),
- embed and index everything, locally.

## 3. Paste the context

The job posting URL, the client brief, the company site. Anchor fetches it, strips it, and builds a **context card**: what they do, their product, their stack, in six anchors. It exists because "so, what do you know about us?" is the question people skip preparing and then get caught by.

## 4. Check your audio

- **Microphone** = you. **System audio (loopback)** = them.
- Anchor shows a live level meter for both. **Test with the exact headset you will use on the call** — especially Bluetooth: some headsets switch profiles mid-call and the levels change.
- If the "them" meter is silent, stop and fix it. Nothing else in the app will work.

## 5. Place the window

Drag the overlay so it sits **directly under your webcam**. Not below, not to the side — under it. Your eyes barely move, and it reads as looking at the camera. This one habit does more for how you come across than anything else in the app.

## 6. Say the honest sentence

If your jurisdiction or your conscience calls for it, open the call with one line: "I keep live notes on my side." Anchor has no stealth mode on purpose — and there is a **Show notes** button if you ever want to put your cards on screen for the other side.

## 7. Take the call

- The card follows the topic — from the first words, theirs or yours.
- The next anchor you have not covered is highlighted; covered anchors dim.
- Unexpected question? Anchor assembles a card from **your own material** and marks it amber. If it had to reach beyond your material, those bullets carry a violet mark — you always know what is yours and what is not.
- Stuck? Hit the panic key: clarify, structure, bridge.

## 8. Afterwards

- Read the transcript — text, in whatever language was spoken. No audio was stored; there was none to store.
- Read the **coverage report**: which anchors you hit, which you missed.
- Mark the session green or red.
- **Take the misses back to Step 1.**

That last line is the whole point. The report is not a scoreboard, it is your next study list.
